## LinkedIn

What is one more dollar of official statistics worth? Hayami and Peterson estimated it for USDA crop and livestock surveys in 1971: over $600 in welfare per dollar for cutting sampling error from 2.5 to 2.0 percent. Several later estimates are aggregate ratios instead: about 4 to 6 per dollar for the New Zealand, Australian and UK censuses, and about $25 of GDP per BLS dollar in the new Bloom, Groshen, Hobbs and Strain paper.

An MVPF for a statistic needs willingness to pay for the information over its net fiscal cost. The MVPF paper itself works one piece of that: a policymaker deciding on food stamps would pay 24 cents per dollar of spending to decide with the precision of linked Census data instead of a survey's. In an incomplete search, I have not found anyone who has taken that to a statistical program's marginal spending across all its uses.
